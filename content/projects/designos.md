### Snapshot
DesignOS is the internal UX operating system I shaped as Senior UX Manager at Appfire, co-leading a 35+ person distributed org across BigPicture, 7pace, and AI workstreams. It turns intake, velocity-based estimation, Figma and standards governance, and research ops into shared infrastructure, with an AI-assisted Sense → Frame → Agent → Craft → Ship → Learn loop where Craft and Ship are human gates.

### Problem
Demand arrived in Slack. Estimates lived in people’s heads. Figma libraries and research insights did not travel across squads, and engineering needed a UX status they could schedule against. With AI drafts entering the workflow, craft review risked being skipped. The org needed an operating system, not more heroics from senior designers.

### Goals & constraints
**Goals**
- Make org health glanceable: open intake, time to estimate, standards coverage, research reuse
- Turn intake → triage → estimate → commit into a shared ritual with completeness scoring
- Govern Figma and design-system contributions with accessibility, token, and docs gates
- Use agents where they compress exploration, and keep Craft and Ship human

**Constraints**
- Dense desktop console for a multi-product UX org; status never color alone
- Agent drafts show lineage and confidence, and Craft review is required before Ship
- It had to fit how squads already worked in Jira, Figma, and Slack

### Process
1. **Diagnose the friction with squad leads** - Talked to UX leads and engineering and PM partners about where work stalled: incomplete briefs, tribal estimates, standards drift, research that never got reused.
2. **Design the operating modules** - Overview, Intake, Estimation, Standards, Research, and an agentic loop. Primary path: new intake → completeness score → triage → estimate → commit.
3. **Choose a shared console over shortcuts** - Spreadsheets and Slack kept failing as a source of truth, and a pure AI auto-ship pipeline would have burned craft integrity.
4. **Critique the loop end to end** - Walked the screens with the leads who would use them weekly and tightened completeness scoring and the Craft gate.

### Key decisions
- Treat UX as **delivery infrastructure** (intake → estimate → craft → handoff → learn), not local heroics.
- Agents compress exploration. **Craft and Ship stay human gates**, with no unsupervised production ship.
- Completeness scoring on intake, so triage judges problem quality rather than ticket volume.
- A standards contribution checklist (accessibility, tokens, docs) before anything merges into the library.

### Solution
1. **Org pulse, Overview**: KPI row, the Sense → Learn pipeline, and Needs attention.
2. **Intake queue & triage**: Completeness score, SLA, priority, and squad, with a problem-framing drawer.
3. **Estimation board**: Velocity bands and commit-to-sprint with risk notes, so engineering can schedule against UX.
4. **Figma / standards hub**: Contribution queue with review gates for design-system governance.
5. **Research ops library**: Insights tagged to products and decisions, for reuse before re-research.
6. **Agentic loop workspace**: Agent drafts with confidence and citations; Craft approves, edits, or rejects before Ship.

### Design system notes
Quiet enterprise: cool-gray canvas, indigo accent only. Components: OpsNav, KpiRow, PipelineSteps, CompletenessChecklist, VelocityLane, ContributionReview, InsightCard, CraftGateModal. Pattern: status never color alone, dense tables keyboard-reachable.

### Outcomes & learnings
- **100% team adoption**: not only the UX org, but the whole product trio (design, product, and engineering) works through DesignOS.
- **What changed beyond adoption:** clearer planning for engineering, shared Figma standards, a research-ops habit, and fewer late redesigns on critical paths. These are qualitative outcomes from the org I co-led, not a measured study.
- **Learning:** agents only earn trust when Craft and Ship stay human gates.
